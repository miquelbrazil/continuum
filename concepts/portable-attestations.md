# Professional portability and attestations

- Status: draft concept and use-case family.
- Origin: private design dialogue; generalized with AI assistance.
- Related: [RFC 0001](../rfcs/0001-continuum.md), [privacy](privacy-and-projections.md), [provenance](provenance-and-confidence.md).

A work ledger can preserve a person's contribution history, decisions, case
studies, outcomes, and review or promotion evidence across institutions. Claims
must distinguish self-report, collaborator observation, and institution-issued
attestation. A future employer receives a reviewed professional projection, not
an employer's confidential source material or the subject's whole corpus.

A provider scenario makes the boundary concrete: recurring feedback from
colleagues, patients, and institutions could inform a clinician's longitudinal
professional record. HR, medical staff, patient experience, or quality groups
are possible organizational partners, not validated buyers. A patient-facing
interview or Oracle would need an even narrower projection. Trajectory,
context, role, sample, and uncertainty matter more than one practitioner score.
Feedback should be contestable and should not silently become a durable
identity judgment.

Portable attestations need issuer, subject, claim, context, issue date, scope,
expiry or status, and a correction or revocation path. Proof of issuance and
integrity is different from permission to disclose underlying evidence. Keep
personal records off-chain. Compare signatures and status services, content
addressing, transparency logs, Verifiable Credentials, selective disclosure,
and only then a blockchain where decentralized trust adds a concrete property.
Public commitments can still leak associations or correlate people.
