# Enrichment and transparent elicitation

- Status: draft concept.
- Origin: private design dialogue and recordings; generalized with AI assistance.
- Related: [RFC 0003](../rfcs/0003-consentful-enrichment.md), [provenance](provenance-and-confidence.md), [privacy](privacy-and-projections.md).

Enrichment can be **passive** (organize permitted existing material),
**responsive** (ask when an answer exposes a consequential gap), or
**intentional** (a subject chooses an area to explore). Candidate sources
include messages, email, calls, repositories, documents, calendars, social
feeds, and surveys. A connector requires a declared scope and purpose;
connecting a service is not blanket permission to ingest every record or infer
every trait.

The interpretive loop is **collect → observe → hypothesize → ask → clarify →
distill → confirm → revisit**. Questions can be selected for information gain,
but the subject should know why they are being asked and what a response will
affect. The model should negotiate understanding with the person, not silently
define them. Naturalistic behavior, self-report, witness observation,
institutional evidence, elicited response, and model inference remain distinct
evidence kinds.

A design exploration considered an account that would quietly post tailored
stimuli and read reactions as signals. That path exposed the consent problem: a
person may know an app holds data without knowing that it is experimentally
steering their feed. The working boundary is **transparent experimentation with
the subject, for the subject**. No covert fake accounts, hidden social tests,
or pressure through social relationships. If an elicitation is run, its
identity, purpose, interpretation, and withdrawal path should be visible.

Witness nomination, invitation, opt-in, contribution, subject review, and
disclosure are separate states. Reciprocity is optional. The system must not
turn contact import into unsolicited invitations or non-consensual enrollment.
